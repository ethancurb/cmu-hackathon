# Strategy every agent must know

Win a track by delivering a distinctive, useful result that judges can understand and verify in three minutes. Optimize the whole outcome: technical work, integration, proof, presentation, and submission. More generated code is not evidence of progress.

## Turn the rubric into evidence

The criteria below are official; the evidence suggestions are our strategy. [Deck](../HackCMU%202026%20Opening%20Ceremony.pdf), slide 35.

| Criterion | Give the judge something concrete |
| --- | --- |
| Originality | Show the fresh mechanism or interaction and how it differs from the current workaround. |
| Technical difficulty | Demonstrate one real engineering challenge and its solution. The deck explicitly contrasts this with a ChatGPT wrapper. Explain the tradeoff; a stack list proves little. |
| Demo quality | A clear, readable, repeatable input → result within the three-minute presentation. |
| Usefulness | Show who benefits, the need, and a completed useful action or credible comparison. |
| Track relevance | Make the selected theme central to the core interaction and the submission explanation. |

## Choose and refine the idea

Name one user, one situation, one observable improvement, and one memorable technical centerpiece. A game or social experience can meet a real need; a startup business model is not an extra published criterion. A model brand or generic chat interface is insufficient differentiation.

Run a short real-input experiment on the hardest assumption before building around it. Verify the needed data, device, API, latency, or algorithm. Compare the idea and at most two narrower versions using [the idea review](RESEARCH.md#idea-review). Use evidence and explicit unknowns; never invent rubric weights or win probabilities.

Keep at most three must-have outcomes. Every addition must justify its total build, review, integration, and demo cost and name what it replaces. Preserve the technical centerpiece when cutting scope. A reliable but indistinguishable app also leaves rubric points unearned.

Choose the strongest genuine track fit. Fewer entrants do not establish better odds: prize allocation and competitor quality are uncertain. Add a sponsor entry only when its technology improves this same experience and its requirements are verified. [Track analysis](RESEARCH.md#track-and-sponsor-choice).

## Execute toward proof

Build a rough complete flow early; test across the actual component boundary. Work from the same contracts and fixtures. Do not let four convincing individual demos substitute for one integrated product. [Execution rules](../AGENTS.md).

Plan backward from submission using the remaining time. Our default checkpoints and cut triggers are in [the schedule](RESEARCH.md#build-and-proof-schedule); record agreed cutoffs in [PROJECT](PROJECT.md). Reserve the final hours for reliability, presentation, and delivery. A passed checkpoint needs evidence, not a status label.

For each GitHub issue, state briefly: **criterion/outcome → visible proof → changed scope → verification**. People choose their own work in [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/ethancurb/cmu-hackathon/issues); personal ledgers preserve published outcomes under [AGENTS](../AGENTS.md). Follow the chosen win thesis within that task; propose a shared pivot with evidence, full cost, and what it replaces.

Use [DEMO](DEMO.md) to rehearse on the presentation machine. Show actual behavior, label cached/fixture/recorded fallbacks, and qualify measurements. Historic winners demonstrate possibilities; they do not validate clinical claims, model accuracy, or a guaranteed formula. Read [RESEARCH](RESEARCH.md) selectively when choosing ideas, resolving assumptions, or changing strategy.
