## Design Context

### Users

Pittsburgh and CMU-area transit riders using LoadLine on a phone while deciding how to travel and when to leave. The interface must also communicate its model and evidence quickly during a short live judging demo.

### Brand Personality

Calm, civic, and assured. LoadLine should feel like a precise transit instrument with an editorial character: useful under time pressure, candid about uncertainty, and never sensational.

### Aesthetic Direction

A warm, light, refined transit interface: warm canvas, serif display type, compact mono utilities, thin borders, square controls, deep blue ink, and restrained pressure colors. The map or vehicle model is the primary visual surface; route information belongs in concise, scannable tiles directly below it. Avoid generic dashboard cards, glossy gradients, neon tech styling, and decorative motion.

### Design Principles

1. Put the travel decision first: map or vehicle context, then selectable route tiles, then deeper evidence.
2. Keep current observations, forecasts, and model indices visually and verbally distinct.
3. Prefer a few strong, visible choices over redundant modes or hidden duplicate information.
4. Preserve mobile reachability, keyboard focus, reduced-motion behavior, and honest loading/failure states.
5. Make hierarchy through typography, spacing, and thin rules—not visual effects.
